How Search Engines Work

The internet is like a giant spider web, with webpages and pieces of content acting like insects caught in its threads. Just as a spider moves across its web to locate and gather information about what has been trapped, search engines send out automated programs called “crawlers.” These crawlers travel across the web, discovering and indexing content. They then organize and store that information so that when someone searches for a topic, the search engine can quickly provide the most relevant results.

The Importance of Keywords and Descriptions as Meta Tags

Keywords and descriptions are important meta tags because they help search engines understand what a webpage is about. Keywords identify the main topics of a page, while meta descriptions provide a brief summary of their content. These elements can improve a page's relevance in search results and encourage users to click on the page when it appears in search engine listings. Search engines use information like this when organizing indexed content and presenting relevant results to users.

The Four Elements that Comprise Writing Relevant Content Links

To create effective and relevant content, SEO professionals often follow the E-E-A-T framework: Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, and Trustworthiness. Experience comes from firsthand knowledge of a topic, while expertise reflects a strong understanding of the subject matter. Authoritativeness is established when others recognize a website as a credible source of information. Trustworthiness is demonstrated by consistently providing accurate and reliable content. Together, these qualities help search engines identify high-quality content and increase its visibility in search results.

What Constitutes Good Heading Hierarchy

Good heading hierarchy begins with an H1 heading for the main topic of a page and then progresses to H2, H3, and lower-level headings as needed. This structure helps organize content logically, making it easier for both users and search engines to understand the relationship between sections and subsections. Headings should follow a clear order and should not skip levels unnecessarily.

Example:

  • The title of this page: WDDE 375 - Project 1 is an H1 heading.
  • The subheading: How Search Engines Work is an H2 heading.
  • The subheading: The Importance of Keywords and Descriptions as Meta Tags is an H3 heading.
  • The subheading: The Four Elements that Comprise Writing Relevant Content Links is an H4 heading.
  • The subheading: What Constitutes Good Heading Hierarchy is an H5 heading.

This hierarchy shows how major topics are divided into smaller, related sections, creating a clear and organized structure for readers.
